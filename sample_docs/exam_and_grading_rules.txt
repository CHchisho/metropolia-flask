Examination and Assessment Regulations - Example University of Applied Sciences (fictional sample document)

1. Assessment methods

Courses are assessed through exams, assignments, projects, portfolios, or a combination of these. The assessment method and the weight of each component are stated in the course description published before enrolment opens.

2. Grading scale

Courses are graded on a scale from 0 to 5. Grade 5 is excellent, 4 is very good, 3 is good, 2 is satisfactory, 1 is passable and 0 is fail. Some courses, such as practical training, use a pass/fail assessment instead. The teacher must publish the results within 21 calendar days of the exam or the assignment deadline.

3. Exams

Students must register for each exam at least seven days in advance. Bring a valid photo ID to every exam. Mobile phones, smart watches and other devices must be switched off and left in your bag unless the teacher has allowed them. Arriving more than 30 minutes late disqualifies you from the exam. You may not leave the exam room during the first 30 minutes.

Electronic exams are held in the exam centre, which is open Monday to Friday from 8:00 to 18:00. Book a time slot at least three days ahead.

4. Retakes

You have three attempts per course in total: the original exam and two retakes. Retakes are organised at least twice per academic year, usually in December, March and May. To improve a passing grade, you may retake an exam once within six months. The better grade is recorded.

5. Late submissions

Assignments submitted after the deadline are graded one grade lower unless you have agreed a new deadline with the teacher beforehand. Submissions more than seven days late are not accepted unless there is a documented medical or other serious reason.

6. Requesting a review

If you disagree with a grade, you can ask the teacher for an explanation within 14 days of the results being published. If you still disagree, submit a written request for rectification to the programme's rectification board within 14 days of receiving the teacher's explanation. The board's decision is final at the university level.

7. Academic integrity

Plagiarism, cheating and unauthorised collaboration are serious violations. All written work may be checked with plagiarism detection software. Using generative AI tools is permitted only when the course description allows it, and any use must be disclosed in the submission. A first violation normally results in a failed grade for the assignment and a warning. Repeated or severe cases are handled by the university's disciplinary committee and can lead to suspension for up to one year.

8. Special arrangements

Students with a learning difficulty, disability or long-term illness may request special arrangements such as extra time, a separate room, or assistive technology. Apply to study counselling at least 14 days before the exam and attach a supporting statement.
